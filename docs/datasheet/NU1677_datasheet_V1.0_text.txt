8 Application Description
8.1 Operation Mode
The device supports three operation modes: normal mode (RX mode and TX mode), standby mode and disable
mode.
The device default enters the normal mode after power up (V5V0 voltage is above V ). The MCU can
V5V0_UVLO
select RX mode, TX mode, standby mode or disable mode according to the AC switching signal, VOUT voltage,
AP I2C commands and PDET_EN signal.
8.2 Power Supply
As shown in Figure 8, the device integrates V5V LDO and V1V8 LDO to provide regulated analog and digital
power supplies. It also integrates capless V1V8 LDO and capless V1V2 LDO to generate individual 1.8V and 1.2V
reference for each GPIO output.
When V5V0 is larger than V and DVDD is larger than V , IC is powered up to turn on internal
V5V0_UVLO DVDD_UVLO
circuit blocks, such as OSC, MCU, protection circuits, rectifier switches and digital circuits.
V5V0 can also be powered up with an external power supply from VDD pin. The power path is controlled by I2C or
the MCU. Only when VDD is above V and VDD Switch supply path is enabled by configuring the register
VDD_UVLO
EN_VDD_PATH to 1, the VDD switch is turned ON and V5V0 follows external VDD voltage through an inner
power path. When VDD drops below V - V or VDD supply path is disabled by any one of
VDD_UVLO VDD_UVLO_HYS
control source, the VDD power path will be turned OFF. Once VDD supply path is enabled, V5V_LDO is turned
OFF.

8.3 Rectifier / Inverter
The rectifier is a key block in charge of converting the AC input power from the receiving coil into a DC supply rail
for the following linear regulator in RX mode. As shown in Figure 9, it consists of four N-channel MOSFETs arranged
in a full bridge. When synchronous rectifier function is enabled, the MOSFETs are driven by a control block which
monitors the AC1 and AC2 voltage, especially for the heavy output load. It has built in a reliable and efficient switch
control algorithm to minimize the dead-time while eliminating the possibility of the shoot-through inside the rectifier.
Moreover, the operating mode of rectifier can be configured to full-bridge mode and half-bridge mode. Once
configured to half-bridge mode, the low side MOSFET Q4 is turned ON, then lower power can be coupled to
generate the same VRECT voltage with same coil and coupling position.
The default working mode of rectifier is half bridge mode. When the receiving coil is placed in the magnetic field
created by the transmitter digital ping, if the VRECT is higher than 1.5V, AC2 low side MOSFET Q4 will be turned
on automatically. Then the full bridge rectifier becomes a half-bridge rectifier, the VRECT voltage will be doubled,
which can extend the ping area. When this pin is connected to GND, the default mode of rectifier is full-bridge
mode.

8.5 Main Linear Regulator
The main linear regulator (MLDO) of the device ensures the constant output voltage with ultra-low drop-out
voltage to minimize the power loss. The output voltage can be programed through I2C interface. The
programmable voltage range is from 3.5V to 23V with 10mV step resolution.
The transistor of MLDO is an N-channel MOSFET and there is no need to add additional bootstrap capacitor. The
MLDO features an outstanding control loop, ensuring excellent line and load regulation performance.
MLDO also has a soft-start feature to prevent in-rush current caused by charging output capacitor during the
startup. The soft start gradually turns on the MLDO to control and limit its current.
The MLDO’s N-MOSFET can be configured to be fully ON in Tx mode.
8.6 In-Band Communication
Robust and reliable in-band FSK demodulation and ASK modulation are critical to the operation of any Qi
compliant Rx devices. The device has dedicated circuits to measure the AC1 and AC2’s flip frequency and the
firmware algorithm to achieve the FSK demodulation function. The device provides two sets of modulation
capacitors, namely CMA1/2 and CMB1/2. These two sets of ASK communication capacitors can be used in
parallel or individually according to the configuration in different applications. Based on the optimal ASK
modulation firmware algorithm, The device can both ensure the performance of in-band control and mitigate the
impact on the VRECT voltage. In addition, the device also integrates enhanced programmable ILOAD ask
communication circuit to ensure the device can pass the eye diagram test of MPP certification.
The device also contains in-band FSK modulation and ASK demodulation. According to configured FSK polarity
and depth, the device can achieve reliable FSK modulation. To reduce the error rate of in-band communication in
Tx mode, the device integrates three synchronous digital demodulation channels (Vpeak/Iac_sns/Isns). As shown
in Figure 11, firstly one of three kinds of signals can be selected for ASK demodulation. Then two kinds of digital
demodulation methods are used to deal with each signal. Finally, there are two digital decoded signals to MCU.
The DEM pin is used as an analog input pin to detect the envelope of the peak voltage of the connecting node
(Vcoil) between the resonant inductor and capacitors.

8.7 Protection
8.7.1 Over-Voltage Protection
Since the feedback loop between Rx and Tx is inherently slow, the transmitter is unable to reduce the power output
instantly when the over-voltage condition occurs on the receiver side. The delay can be in the range of tens or even
hundreds of milli-seconds which is a long time enough to damage the IC. The over-voltage protection circuit
engages immediately upon the occurrence of the over-voltage condition. The device integrates four concurrent OVP
mechanisms based on hardware comparators: OVP2, OVP1, DFT_OVP.
The device allows the MCU to configure the associated set of hardware actions for each OVP. The detailed
actions list refers to Table1.

The current Isns are measured by the integrated high accuracy current measurement circuit across the metal

8.8 ADC
The device integrates an accurate 14bit ADC which takes inputs from internal signals such as VRECT voltage,
VOUT voltage, output current and temperature of die. These signals are used to set the proper VRECT voltage
through communication with the transmitter and achieve accurate and small various power-loss calculations for
FOD.
8.9 I2C, nINT
The device allows for I2C communication with both SCL and SDA. The default I2C address is 0x34(7bit).
nINT pin is an open-drain and low active pin. A resistor is needed to add between this pin and pull-up supply rail
(V5V0 or V1P8 or V1P2). This pin is pulled high during normal mode. Under protection conditions or occurrence
of other reportable events, the nINT pin is pulled low to indicate a fault condition. The detail information can be
read through the I2C interface.

8.10 GPIO and OD
The device integrates 4 general purpose I/O pins and 1 open-drain I/O pins. The GPIO pins can be configured
both as inputs and outputs according to configuration. As output, the GPIO pins support push-pull and open-drain
mode and the OD pins can only support open-drain mode. The high-level output voltage of the GPIO pins can be
configured as V1P8 and V1P2 according to MCU configuration. As input, the GPIO pins support internal pull-up,
pull-down and high-Z mode, and the OD pins only support pull-down and high-Z mode. The input thresholds of
high-level and low-level also can be configured by the MCU to match the external input voltage rail.

decaying. The ringing of the coil voltage is sensed by QS pin and processed by internal analog blocks to calculate

8.12 Ping Detect Function
The device integrates an ultra-low-power ping detection function. Once the RX device is fully charged or
overheated, AP can enable the Ping Detect function via output a high-level signal to PDET_EN pin. As shown in
Figure 14, PDET_EN pin also provides power to the ping detect module, with a power consumption of less than
20uA. Once the MCU receives the enable signal, it will configure the timeout duration threshold of ping detect
function and store the data in the registers. After that, it can pull down the PDETB signal to notify the AP that it is
ready to enter Ping detect mode. Then the device sends an EPT (End of Packet Transmission) to signal the TX
(transmitter) to stop charging. When the chip loses power(V5V0 is below V ), the timer counter begins to
V5V0_UVLO
count. Normally, the interval between TX digital pings is less than the timeout duration threshold value, so when
the next digital ping occurs, the device powers up and the count value is less than the threshold. The PDETB
remains pulled low to indicate that the TX is present. This continues until the RX is removed from the TX, and
after the power-down following the last digital ping, the count value exceeds the threshold. At this point, the
PDETB is released high to inform the AP that the TX is not present. The example waveform is shown in Figure
15.
The Ping detect function can also be used in cloak ping function for MPP.
